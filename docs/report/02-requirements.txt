2. REQUIREMENTS SPECIFICATION

2.1 User roles

The brief asks for at least three categories of user. TriageGraph has five, because a campus IT
department has team leads and an IT manager as well as technicians and administrators.

  Role            Who                                   Main goals
  --------------  ------------------------------------  ---------------------------------------------
  Employee        Students, faculty and staff           Report a problem, follow it, answer questions,
                                                        confirm the fix, give feedback
  Technician      Support engineers                     Work an assigned queue: acknowledge,
                                                        investigate, note, resolve
  Team lead       Senior technician in charge of a      Everything a technician can do, plus see the
                  team                                  team's queue and reassign; first escalation
                                                        target
  Administrator   System administrators                 Manage users, technicians, services,
                                                        categories, SLA policies and strategies;
                                                        monitor everything
  IT manager      Head of the IT department             Read-only oversight: Command Centre, reports,
                                                        audit; final escalation target

Self-registration always creates an Employee. Every other account is created by an Administrator.


2.2 Functional requirements

Accounts and access
  FR-01  A visitor can register as an Employee with name, email, department and password.
  FR-02  A user can log in with email and password and receives a session token valid for 2 hours.
  FR-03  After 5 wrong passwords in a row the account is locked for 5 minutes.
  FR-04  Each role sees its own interface: Employee (blue top bar), Technician and Team lead (teal
         sidebar), Administrator and IT manager (dark indigo sidebar).
  FR-05  An Administrator can create, edit, deactivate and reactivate users of any role.
  FR-06  An Administrator can set a technician's skills, experience, maximum workload, availability
         and team lead.

Incident reporting and lifecycle
  FR-07  An Employee can report an incident with title, description, affected service, impact and
         urgency. Voice dictation is offered where the browser supports it.
  FR-08  Each incident gets a readable code (INC-1024), the reporter, category, service, impact,
         urgency, priority, creation time, status, assignee, SLA deadlines, notes, resolution and
         closure information.
  FR-09  An incident moves through ten states: NEW, CLASSIFIED, ASSIGNED, ACKNOWLEDGED, IN_PROGRESS,
         PENDING_INFO, RESOLVED, VERIFIED, CLOSED and MERGED. Only the transitions in BR-L1 to BR-L8
         are allowed, and the UI only shows buttons for allowed actions.
  FR-10  The Employee can view their incidents, follow the status, add information, answer a
         technician's question, see the resolution, verify or reopen, and rate the fix from 1 to 5.
  FR-11  A Technician can view assigned incidents, acknowledge, start work, add internal or public
         notes, request information from the reporter, record resolution details and resolve.
  FR-12  A Technician can see their current workload against their maximum.

Classification, priority and assignment
  FR-13  Incidents are classified into data-driven categories (Hardware, Software, Network, Database,
         Cloud, Security, Authentication, Application, Storage and any added later). A new category is
         a new data row, not a code change.
  FR-14  Priority P1 to P4 is calculated from impact, urgency, the service's business criticality,
         the number of affected users and the service's blast radius in the dependency graph.
  FR-15  The system assigns a technician automatically with the active strategy. Four strategies
         exist (skill based, load balanced, round robin, smart score) and an Administrator can switch
         between them at run time.

SLA, escalation and notification
  FR-16  Response and resolution deadlines come from the SLA policy for the service and priority,
         falling back to the default policy.
  FR-17  The system warns at 75% of the time used, records a violation at 100%, and pauses the clock
         while waiting on the reporter.
  FR-18  An unacknowledged incident escalates through four levels: assignee, team lead, system
         administrator, IT manager.
  FR-19  Users are notified of events that concern them through three channels: in-app (live bell
         and inbox), email, and phone push. Which channels are used depends on the event, the
         recipient's role and the priority (see BR-N1).
  FR-20  A user can list their notifications, see the unread count, and mark one or all as read.
  FR-21  An Administrator can send a test notification to any user on every channel and read the
         delivery log (sent, failed or skipped, with the error).

Intelligence
  FR-22  While an Employee types, the system suggests a category and service and warns about likely
         duplicates. The Employee can join an existing incident instead of opening a new one.
  FR-23  A Technician can merge a duplicate into its parent.
  FR-24  Technicians see the most similar resolved incidents and a suggested resolution.
  FR-25  When several services fail together, the dependency graph is used to rank probable root
         causes and a Major Incident can be declared over the related incidents.

Audit, search and reporting
  FR-26  Every change to an incident is written to a tamper-evident audit trail (action, incident,
         previous state, new state, description, actor, time). An Administrator can verify the chain.
  FR-27  Incidents can be searched and filtered by code, date range, status, priority, category,
         service, technician, SLA status, reporter, major incident and free text. Results are paged
         and limited to what the user is allowed to see.
  FR-28  Administrators and the IT manager can view six reports (incident overview, technician
         workload, service analysis, trend, SLA compliance by priority, AI effectiveness) and export
         them as CSV.
  FR-29  The Command Centre shows headline numbers and a live feed of incident events as they
         happen.


2.3 Non-functional requirements

  NFR-01 Performance     Pages load in under 2 s on a laptop with the demo data. Triage suggestions
                         appear in under 1 s. Live notifications reach the browser within 1 s of
                         the event.
  NFR-02 Reliability     A failure in email, push or the AI service never fails or rolls back an
                         incident action. Notification sends are retried 3 times with backoff and
                         then logged as failed.
  NFR-03 Security        See section 11 (Security considerations).
  NFR-04 Usability       Each role has a distinct layout. Errors are shown as readable messages,
                         never stack traces. Every page works at phone width.
  NFR-05 Maintainability Modules talk only through shared contracts or domain events. Every
                         variation point (state, priority calculator, assignment strategy,
                         notification channel, escalation level) is an interface, so a new variant
                         is one new class.
  NFR-06 Testability     Time comes from an injected Clock and external systems sit behind
                         interfaces, so tests are deterministic and run without Docker.
  NFR-07 Scalability     Stateless authentication and module boundaries allow horizontal scaling
                         and a later split into services (see Technology selection, Q9).
  NFR-08 Portability     One command starts the database and mail server (Docker Compose). The
                         backend runs on any machine with Java 21.


2.4 Business rules

Lifecycle
  BR-L1  NEW can be classified (to CLASSIFIED) or merged into another incident (to MERGED).
  BR-L2  CLASSIFIED can be assigned (to ASSIGNED) or merged.
  BR-L3  ASSIGNED can be acknowledged by the assignee only, or reassigned by a team lead or admin.
  BR-L4  ACKNOWLEDGED can be started (to IN_PROGRESS) by the assignee, or reassigned.
  BR-L5  IN_PROGRESS can ask the reporter for information (to PENDING_INFO) or be resolved.
  BR-L6  PENDING_INFO returns to IN_PROGRESS when the reporter replies.
  BR-L7  RESOLVED can be verified by the reporter (to VERIFIED) or reopened with a reason.
  BR-L8  VERIFIED can be closed. CLOSED and MERGED are final; only feedback on a CLOSED incident is
         allowed afterwards. Any other action raises InvalidStateTransitionException.

Priority
  BR-P1  score = impact x urgency x criticality x affected-users multiplier x blast-radius multiplier.
         P1 if score >= 40, P2 if >= 20, P3 if >= 8, otherwise P4.
  BR-P2  A Security incident with impact 3 or more is at least P2. A Major Incident is always P1.
  BR-P3  Joining a duplicate adds one affected user and recalculates priority.

SLA
  BR-S1  Default targets (response / resolution): P1 5 min / 30 min, P2 15 min / 2 h,
         P3 1 h / 8 h, P4 4 h / 24 h. A service-specific policy overrides the default.
  BR-S2  Targets are copied into the incident when it is created; later policy changes do not move
         deadlines on open incidents.
  BR-S3  The resolution clock pauses in PENDING_INFO and RESOLVED; the paused time is added to the
         deadline.
  BR-S4  Each warning and each breach is raised once per incident and kind.

Assignment
  BR-A1  Only active, available technicians below their maximum workload are eligible.
  BR-A2  If skilled technicians exist but all are full, WorkloadExceededException; if nobody has the
         skill, TechnicianUnavailableException. The incident stays CLASSIFIED and the team lead is told.

Duplicates
  BR-D1  Combined similarity 0.75 or more is "likely duplicate"; 0.60 to 0.75 is "possibly related".
  BR-D2  The same reporter submitting near-identical text (similarity 0.95 or more) within 10
         minutes is rejected with a link to the existing incident.

Notification
  BR-N1  Channels by event (recipient: channels):
           Reported                 reporter: in-app, email
           Assigned / reassigned    assignee: in-app, push, plus email if P1; reporter: in-app
           Priority changed         assignee, reporter: in-app
           Information requested    reporter: in-app, email
           SLA warning              assignee: in-app, push
           SLA breached             assignee and team lead: in-app, push, email
           Escalated                escalation target: in-app, push, email
           Resolved                 reporter and followers: in-app, email
           Closed                   reporter: in-app
           Major incident declared  IT manager, admins, team leads: push, email;
                                    affected reporters: in-app
  BR-N2  A user without an email address or push topic is skipped for that channel and the skip is
         logged. A failed send never affects the incident.

Accounts
  BR-U1  Passwords are at least 8 characters with at least one letter and one digit.
  BR-U2  Email addresses are unique.
  BR-U3  Five wrong passwords in a row lock the account for 5 minutes. The error never says whether
         the email or the password was wrong.
